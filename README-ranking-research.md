## Key Findings

### Major Platforms by Level

**High School (USA):**
- **Athletic.net**: Most comprehensive, power ratings, team pages (PRIMARY)
- **MileSplit**: Best media coverage, state-specific sites
- **DyeStat**: National rankings focus

**College (USA):**
- **TFRRS**: NCAA standard for college track & field

**International/Elite:**
- **World Athletics**: Olympic/professional level
- **Power of 10**: UK athletics
- **Tilastopaja**: European/all-time stats

### Ranking Methodologies

**1. Performance-Based**
- Direct time/distance comparison
- Simple and transparent
- Used for single-event rankings
- Distinguishes wind-legal vs. wind-aided

**2. Points-Based (IAAF Tables)**
- Converts performances to standardized points
- Enables cross-event comparison
- Scientific/statistical foundation
- Used for multi-events and all-around rankings

**3. Power Ratings**
- Algorithm considers competition strength
- Rewards consistency
- More predictive of championship success
- Proprietary to specific platforms

**4. Team Rankings**
- Aggregates individual performances
- Various scoring methods
- Virtual meet projections
- Depth analysis

### Critical Success Factors

**For Programs Using Rankings:**

## Best Practices Summary

### DO:
✅ Use rankings as one measure of success among many
✅ Maintain data quality and accuracy
✅ Set realistic, progressive goals
✅ Balance elite and participation focus
✅ Celebrate achievements at all levels
✅ Integrate rankings into planning systematically
✅ Protect athlete privacy and well-being
✅ Monitor trends, not just positions
✅ Use rankings to inform decisions
✅ Maintain ethical competition practices

### DON'T:
❌ Make rankings the sole success measure
❌ Sacrifice development for ranking points
❌ Ignore non-ranked athletes
❌ Manipulate competition selection purely for rankings
❌ Create excessive pressure around rankings
❌ Neglect data quality
❌ Compare without context
❌ Use rankings to shame or demotivate
❌ Violate privacy standards
❌ Over-invest at expense of fundamentals
