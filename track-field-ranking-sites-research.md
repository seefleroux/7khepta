# Track and Field Ranking Sites Research

## Executive Summary

This document provides comprehensive research on track and field ranking sites, their methodologies, features, and how they work. This research is intended to inform strategic planning for high school track and field programs and help understand how performance benchmarking, talent identification, and competitive positioning work in the athletics ecosystem.

## Major Track and Field Ranking Sites

### 1. World Athletics Rankings (formerly IAAF)
**Website:** worldathletics.org
**Scope:** International elite athletes
**Focus:** Professional and Olympic-level competition

#### How It Works:
- **Points-based system**: Athletes earn points based on performance levels and competition standards
- **Performance tables**: Uses scientifically-derived performance tables that assign point values to marks/times
- **Competition weighting**: Different competitions receive different weighting (Olympics, World Championships get highest)
- **Time decay**: Recent performances weighted more heavily than older ones
- **Rolling period**: Typically uses performances from the past 12 months
- **Event-specific rankings**: Separate rankings for each track and field event

#### Methodology:
- Uses statistical analysis to create equivalency between performances
- Considers the quality of competition faced
- Adjusts for altitude (for certain events)
- Regular updates throughout the competitive season
- Transparent scoring tables available publicly

### 2. Athletic.net
**Website:** athletic.net
**Scope:** U.S. high school and youth athletics
**Focus:** Comprehensive high school rankings and results database

#### How It Works:
- **Results aggregation**: Collects meet results from thousands of competitions
- **Power ratings**: Proprietary algorithm that ranks athletes and teams
- **Historical database**: Maintains extensive historical performance data
- **Team rankings**: Aggregates individual performances into team scores
- **State and national rankings**: Multiple levels of competitive comparison

#### Key Features:
- Meet results posting and management
- Athlete profiles with complete performance history
- Season rankings (updated in real-time)
- All-time performance lists
- Comparison tools between athletes/teams
- Video integration for performances
- Performance analysis tools
- College recruiting integration

#### Ranking Methodology:
- Considers strength of competition
- Adjusts for meet type and level
- Weights recent performances
- Considers consistency of performance
- Separate rankings by grade/age division
- Wind-legal vs. wind-aided distinctions

### 3. MileSplit
**Website:** milesplit.com
**Scope:** U.S. high school track and cross country
**Focus:** Media coverage combined with rankings

#### How It Works:
- **State-specific sites**: Network of state-level platforms
- **Performance rankings**: Lists top performers by event
- **Virtual meets**: Allows comparison across different competitions
- **Editorial rankings**: Combines statistical data with expert analysis
- **Video and media integration**: Extensive race footage and coverage

#### Features:
- Live results and timing integration
- Race videos and highlights
- National and state rankings
- Recruiting information
- Training logs and athlete pages
- Fantasy cross country/track
- Mobile app for real-time updates

### 4. TFRRS (Track & Field Results Reporting System)
**Website:** tfrrs.org
**Scope:** U.S. collegiate track and field
**Focus:** NCAA and college athletics

#### How It Works:
- **Meet results database**: Comprehensive college meet results
- **Descending order lists**: Rankings based purely on performance marks
- **Team scoring**: Projects team scores based on entered meets
- **Conference rankings**: Division-specific rankings
- **Qualifying standards**: Tracks athletes against championship qualifying marks

#### Methodology:
- Direct performance comparisons
- Season-best and personal-best tracking
- Conference affiliation consideration
- Championship qualifying list management
- Historical performance archives

### 5. Tilastopaja (Finnish Athletics)
**Website:** tilastopaja.eu
**Scope:** International, particularly European athletics
**Focus:** Comprehensive statistical database

#### How It Works:
- **All-time rankings**: Extensive historical database
- **Multi-level rankings**: From youth to elite
- **Seasonal rankings**: Current season performance lists
- **Country-specific rankings**: National-level performance tracking
- **Age-group rankings**: Youth and junior categories

### 6. Power of 10 (UK Athletics)
**Website:** thepowerof10.info
**Scope:** United Kingdom athletics
**Focus:** All levels from youth to elite

#### How It Works:
- **Results database**: Comprehensive UK competition results
- **Athlete profiles**: Individual performance histories
- **Club rankings**: Team/club performance tracking
- **Age-grading**: Adjusts performances for age
- **Rankings by age group**: Youth through veteran categories

#### Key Features:
- Over 20 years of historical data
- Competition calendars
- Club information and statistics
- Cross-reference capabilities
- Free access to most features

### 7. DyeStat
**Website:** dyestat.com
**Scope:** U.S. high school track and cross country
**Focus:** News, rankings, and results

#### Features:
- National and state rankings
- Meet results and calendars
- Recruiting information
- Performance lists
- Historical archives

## Common Ranking Methodologies

### Performance-Based Rankings
**Principle:** Direct comparison of marks/times
- Fastest time wins (running events)
- Longest/highest distance wins (field events)
- Adjusted for wind conditions (sprints, horizontal jumps)
- Separate rankings for indoor/outdoor
- Hand-timed vs. fully automatic timing distinctions

**Considerations:**
- Wind legal vs. wind-aided performances
- Altitude adjustments (for certain events)
- Track certification requirements
- Meet certification levels
- Measurement accuracy standards

### Points-Based Systems
**Principle:** Convert performances to standardized point values

**Common Scoring Tables:**
- **IAAF Scoring Tables**: International standard for elite athletics
- **Age-graded scoring**: Adjusts for athlete age
- **Decathlon/Heptathlon scoring**: Multi-event equivalency
- **Youth scoring tables**: Age-appropriate conversions

**Applications:**
- Comparing across events (multi-event rankings)
- Team scoring in meets
- All-around athlete rankings
- Historical comparisons
- Cross-gender comparisons (with appropriate tables)

### Power Rankings/Rating Systems
**Principle:** Algorithm-based ratings considering multiple factors

**Factors Typically Considered:**
- Raw performance (time/distance/height)
- Strength of competition
- Meet level/importance
- Consistency
- Recent form vs. seasonal performance
- Head-to-head results
- Improvement trends

**Examples:**
- Athletic.net power ratings
- Elo-style rating systems
- Computer-generated rankings
- Predictive performance models

### Composite/Team Rankings
**Principle:** Aggregate individual performances to rank teams

**Methodologies:**
- Top N athletes per event
- Scoring based on place (e.g., 10-8-6-5-4-3-2-1)
- Depth scoring (all qualifying athletes count)
- Virtual meet projections
- Season-long point accumulation
- Dual meet records

## Conclusion

Track and field ranking sites serve multiple crucial functions in the athletics ecosystem:

1. **Performance Documentation**: Creating permanent, searchable records of athletic achievement
2. **Competitive Context**: Allowing athletes and teams to understand their position in the competitive landscape
3. **Motivation**: Providing goals and benchmarks that drive improvement
4. **Talent Identification**: Helping scouts, recruiters, and coaches find and develop talent
5. **Strategic Planning**: Informing decisions about training, competition, and program development
6. **Community Building**: Creating connections among athletes, coaches, and fans

For high school track and field programs, engaging with ranking sites thoughtfully can:
- Enhance program visibility and reputation
- Support athlete development and motivation
- Inform strategic planning and resource allocation
- Connect the program to the broader athletics community
- Provide data for continuous improvement

However, it's important to maintain balance—rankings should inform decisions but not become the sole measure of success. Programs should emphasize:
- Long-term athlete development
- Educational values
- Inclusive participation
- Ethical competition
- Holistic student-athlete welfare

By understanding how ranking sites work and leveraging them appropriately, high school programs can enhance their strategic planning while maintaining focus on their core mission of developing young athletes.
